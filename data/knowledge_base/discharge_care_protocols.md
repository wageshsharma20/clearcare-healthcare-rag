# Clinical Reference Guide: Hospital Discharge Protocols & Patient Recovery Instructions
*Source: Joint Commission Hospital Quality & Patient Safety Measures, CMS Discharge Guidelines*
*Target Audience: Post-Hospitalization Patient Guidance & Nursing Review*

## 1. General Post-Discharge Recovery Principles
Hospital discharge represents a vulnerable transition period. Adverse events occur in approximately 20% of patients within 30 days of leaving the hospital, with medication discrepancies and unrecognized infection being the leading causes.

---

## 2. Red Flag Symptoms Requiring Immediate Emergency Care (Call 911 / Go to ED)
Patients and caregivers must immediately seek emergency evaluation if any of the following occur:
- **Cardiovascular / Pulmonary:**
  - Sudden, crushing chest pain or pressure radiating to the jaw, neck, left arm, or back.
  - Acute severe shortness of breath or sudden inability to speak in full sentences.
  - Coughing up pink frothy sputum or frank blood.
  - Sudden fainting (syncope) or loss of consciousness.
- **Neurological (Signs of Stroke - FAST):**
  - Sudden facial droop, arm weakness or numbness, or slurred/incomprehensible speech.
  - Sudden severe "thunderclap" headache unlike any previous headache.
- **Thromboembolic (Blood Clot / DVT / PE):**
  - Asymmetric swelling, intense warmth, redness, and tenderness in one calf or thigh.

---

## 3. Urgent Warning Signs Requiring Same-Day Clinic Contact
- **Infection Markers:**
  - Temperature >= 100.4°F (38.0°C) or chills.
  - Surgical incision showing expanding erythema (redness spreading > 1 inch from incision edge), warmth, increased swelling, or purulent (yellow/green/foul-smelling) drainage.
- **Fluid & Gastrointestinal:**
  - Inability to keep fluids down for > 24 hours due to nausea or vomiting.
  - Rapid unexplained weight gain (> 3 lbs in 24 hours or 5 lbs in 1 week), especially in heart failure patients (flags fluid retention).
  - Dark, tarry, black stools (melena) or bright red rectal bleeding.

---

## 4. Medication Reconciliation & Safe Administration Rules
- **Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT) Post-Cardiac Stent:**
  - If a coronary stent was placed, medications like Aspirin plus Clopidogrel (Plavix), Ticagrelor (Brilinta), or Prasugrel MUST NEVER be stopped abruptly without consulting the interventional cardiologist. Abrupt cessation carries a high risk of catastrophic acute stent thrombosis.
- **Pain Management (Opioids vs. NSAIDs):**
  - Do not exceed 3,000 mg of Acetaminophen (Tylenol) in 24 hours from all sources combined (many prescription opioid combination pills, such as Norco or Percocet, already contain 325 mg of Acetaminophen).
  - Avoid NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Naproxen) if patient has kidney disease, history of stomach ulcers, or is taking blood thinners.
- **Antibiotic Stewardship:**
  - Complete the full prescribed course of antibiotics even if feeling 100% improved, unless instructed otherwise by a physician.

---

## 5. Activity Restrictions & Wound Care
- **Lifting Restrictions:** No lifting objects heavier than 10 lbs (roughly a gallon of milk) for 2 to 4 weeks post-abdominal or cardiac surgery.
- **Showering vs. Bathing:** Showering with mild soap and gentle patting dry is generally permitted after 48 hours; do NOT submerge incision in baths, pools, or hot tubs until cleared by the surgeon.
