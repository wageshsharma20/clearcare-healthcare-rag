# Clinical Reference Guide: Lipid Panel & Cardiovascular Health Markers
*Source: American College of Cardiology (ACC) / American Heart Association (AHA) Multisociety Guideline on the Management of Blood Cholesterol*
*Target Audience: Patient Guidance & Primary Care Interpretation*

## 1. Overview of the Lipid Panel
The Lipid Panel is a blood screening test that measures concentrations of fat molecules (lipids) in the bloodstream to calculate cardiovascular risk for heart attack, atherosclerosis, and stroke.

---

## 2. Cholesterol Fractions & Diagnostic Targets

### Total Cholesterol
- **Desirable:** Less than 200 mg/dL
- **Borderline High:** 200 to 239 mg/dL
- **High:** 240 mg/dL or higher
- **Clinical Note:** Total cholesterol reflects LDL + HDL + 20% of Triglycerides. High total cholesterol alone is not an absolute disease diagnosis, but a signal to calculate composite 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk.

### Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL-C) — "Bad Cholesterol"
- **Optimal:** Less than 100 mg/dL
- **Optimal for High-Risk Individuals (History of CAD, Stents, Diabetes):** Less than 70 mg/dL (or < 55 mg/dL for very high risk)
- **Near Optimal / Above Optimal:** 100 to 129 mg/dL
- **Borderline High:** 130 to 159 mg/dL
- **High:** 160 to 189 mg/dL
- **Very High:** 190 mg/dL or higher (Often flags familial hypercholesterolemia genetic screening)
- **Pathophysiology:** Excess LDL particles deposit into coronary and carotid arterial walls, forming inflammatory plaques that can rupture and cause myocardial infarction (heart attack) or ischemic stroke.

### High-Density Lipoprotein (HDL-C) — "Good Cholesterol"
- **Desirable (Protective):**
  - Adult Males: Greater than 40 mg/dL (preferably > 50 mg/dL)
  - Adult Females: Greater than 50 mg/dL (preferably > 60 mg/dL)
- **Low HDL (< 40 mg/dL):** An independent risk factor for coronary artery disease.
- **How to raise HDL:** Aerobic exercise, quitting smoking, consuming healthy monounsaturated fats (olive oil, avocados, nuts).

### Triglycerides
- **Normal:** Less than 150 mg/dL
- **Borderline High:** 150 to 199 mg/dL
- **High:** 200 to 499 mg/dL
- **Very High (Severe Hypertriglyceridemia):** 500 mg/dL or higher
- **Critical Risk:** Levels above 500-1000 mg/dL increase risk of acute pancreatitis.
- **Common Lifestyle Drivers:** Excessive refined sugar/simple carbohydrates, alcohol intake, obesity, untreated hypothyroidism, or uncontrolled diabetes.

## 3. Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c) — Diabetic Marker
- **Normal (Non-Diabetic):** Below 5.7%
- **Prediabetes:** 5.7% to 6.4%
- **Diabetes Diagnostic Threshold:** 6.5% or higher
- **Clinical Action for Prediabetes:** 5-7% intentional body weight loss, 150 minutes/week of moderate physical activity (brisk walking), reduction of sugar-sweetened beverages.
