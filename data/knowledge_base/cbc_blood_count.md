# Clinical Reference Guide: Complete Blood Count (CBC) with Differential
*Source: NIH National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) & CDC Reference Lab*
*Target Audience: Patient Guidance & Primary Care Interpretation*

## 1. Overview of the Complete Blood Count
The Complete Blood Count (CBC) evaluates cellular components circulating in blood: Red Blood Cells (oxygen transport), White Blood Cells (immune defense), and Platelets (blood clotting).

---

## 2. Red Blood Cell Indices (Anemia & Oxygen Delivery)

### Red Blood Cell Count (RBC)
- **Standard Reference Range:**
  - Adult Males: 4.35 to 5.65 million cells/mcL
  - Adult Females: 3.92 to 5.13 million cells/mcL
- **Low Count:** Anemia, active blood loss, bone marrow suppression, nutritional deficiencies (Iron, B12, Folate).
- **High Count (Erythrocytosis):** Chronic low oxygen (smoking, COPD, sleep apnea), high altitude, polycythemia vera, or hemoconcentration due to dehydration.

### Hemoglobin (Hgb)
- **Standard Reference Range:**
  - Adult Males: 13.2 to 16.6 g/dL
  - Adult Females: 11.6 to 15.0 g/dL
- **Mild Anemia (10.0 - 11.5 g/dL):** May cause mild fatigue, pale skin, slight shortness of breath with strenuous exertion.
- **Moderate to Severe Anemia (< 8.0 g/dL):** Palpitations, dizziness, resting shortness of breath. Requires physician evaluation.
- **Critical Threshold (< 7.0 g/dL):** Typically warrants hospital admission and packed red blood cell transfusion evaluation.

### Hematocrit (Hct)
- **Standard Reference Range:**
  - Adult Males: 38.3% to 48.6%
  - Adult Females: 35.5% to 44.9%
- **Definition:** The percentage of total blood volume made up of red blood cells (typically ~3 times the hemoglobin level).

### Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 80 to 96 fL (femtoliters)
- **Microcytic (MCV < 80 fL):** Small red cells. Primary causes: Iron deficiency anemia, Thalassemia trait, Anemia of chronic disease.
- **Normocytic (MCV 80 - 96 fL):** Acute blood loss, kidney failure, early nutritional deficiency.
- **Macrocytic (MCV > 100 fL):** Large red cells. Primary causes: Vitamin B12 deficiency, Folate deficiency, alcoholism, liver disease, hypothyroid state, certain chemotherapies.

## 3. White Blood Cells & Differential (Immune System)

### Total White Blood Cell Count (WBC)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 4,000 to 11,000 cells/mcL (4.0 - 11.0 x 10^3 / mcL)
- **Leukocytosis (WBC > 11,000):** Bacterial infection, acute viral infection, systemic inflammation, severe physical/emotional stress, steroid medication (e.g. Prednisone). Mild elevation (11,000 - 14,000) during or right after an upper respiratory infection or dental work is common and reactive.
- **Leukopenia (WBC < 4,000):** Viral infections (such as influenza, COVID-19, HIV), autoimmune diseases (lupus), bone marrow disorders, or drug side effects.
- **Critical Threshold (< 1,500):** Substantial risk of opportunistic infection; requires isolation precautions.

### Neutrophils (Absolute & Percentage)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 40% to 70% (1,500 - 7,500 cells/mcL)
- **Role:** First responders against bacterial infections. "Left shift" or presence of immature bands suggests acute bacterial response.

### Lymphocytes
- **Standard Reference Range:** 20% to 40% (1,000 - 4,800 cells/mcL)
- **Role:** Combat viral pathogens (T cells, B cells, NK cells). Elevated during mononucleosis, viral illnesses, or chronic lymphocytic conditions.

### Eosinophils
- **Standard Reference Range:** 1% to 4% (50 - 500 cells/mcL)
- **Elevated in:** Allergic conditions (asthma, eczema, hay fever), parasitic infections, eosinophilic esophagitis, or drug reactions.

## 4. Platelets (Coagulation & Clotting)

### Platelet Count
- **Standard Reference Range:** 150,000 to 450,000 cells/mcL
- **Thrombocytopenia (Low Platelets < 150,000):**
  - **100,000 - 149,000:** Mild; often asymptomatic, common in viral recovery or gestational thrombocytopenia.
  - **50,000 - 99,000:** Moderate; minor bruising, petechiae, prolonged bleeding from cuts.
  - **< 20,000:** Severe; high risk of spontaneous mucosal or intracranial bleeding. Urgent medical management needed.
- **Thrombocytosis (High Platelets > 450,000):** Reactive (post-infection, iron deficiency, post-splenectomy) or primary essential thrombocythemia.
