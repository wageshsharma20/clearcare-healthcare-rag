# Clinical Reference Guide: Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP)
*Source: NIH MedlinePlus & Clinical Chemistry Reference Standards (AACC)*
*Target Audience: Patient Guidance & Primary Care Interpretation*

## 1. Overview of the Comprehensive Metabolic Panel
The Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP) is a blood test consisting of 14 separate measurements. It evaluates kidney function, liver function, blood sugar levels, electrolyte balance, and acid/base balance in the blood.

---

## 2. Kidney Function Biomarkers

### Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 7 to 20 mg/dL
- **What it measures:** The amount of nitrogen in the blood that comes from the waste product urea, produced in the liver and eliminated by the kidneys.
- **High Levels (Azotemia):** May indicate impaired renal filtration, acute kidney injury, congestive heart failure, high protein intake, gastrointestinal bleeding, or severe dehydration. Dehydration is the most frequent benign cause of mildly elevated BUN with a normal creatinine.
- **Low Levels:** Seen in severe liver disease, malnutrition, low-protein diets, or overhydration.

### Serum Creatinine
- **Standard Reference Range:** 0.6 to 1.3 mg/dL (adult males: 0.7 - 1.3 mg/dL; adult females: 0.5 - 1.1 mg/dL)
- **What it measures:** A breakdown byproduct of creatine phosphate in skeletal muscle tissue. Because healthy kidneys filter creatinine at a constant rate, it is the primary direct indicator of renal filtering capability.
- **High Levels:** Indicates reduced glomerular filtration, intrinsic kidney disease, urinary tract obstruction, or intense strenuous weightlifting (temporary).
- **Low Levels:** Decreased muscle mass, myasthenia gravis, or advanced age.

### Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)
- **Standard Reference Range:** Greater than 60 mL/min/1.73 m² (Normal baseline: 90 - 120 mL/min/1.73 m²)
- **Interpretation:**
  - **> 90 mL/min:** Normal kidney function.
  - **60 - 89 mL/min:** Mildly decreased kidney filtration; common with normal aging if no protein is in the urine.
  - **45 - 59 mL/min (Stage 3a CKD):** Moderate reduction in kidney function. Requires physician review, medication adjustments, and hydration assessment.
  - **15 - 29 mL/min (Stage 4 CKD):** Severe kidney impairment. Immediate nephrology referral required.
  - **< 15 mL/min:** Kidney failure.

## 3. Electrolytes & Fluid Balance

### Sodium (Na+)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 135 to 145 mEq/L
- **High (Hypernatremia):** Inadequate water intake, excessive sweating, diuretic therapy, diabetes insipidus.
- **Low (Hyponatremia):** Excess water retention, heart failure, cirrhosis, SIADH, vomiting/diarrhea. Mild hyponatremia (130-134 mEq/L) often presents with mild fatigue, nausea, or headache. Severe drops (< 125 mEq/L) are medical emergencies.

### Potassium (K+)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 3.5 to 5.2 mEq/L
- **High (Hyperkalemia):** Kidney dysfunction, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics, tissue trauma. Levels above 6.0 mEq/L can cause fatal cardiac arrhythmias and require urgent attention.
- **Low (Hypokalemia):** Diuretics ("water pills"), prolonged vomiting, diarrhea, insufficient dietary intake. Can cause muscle cramps, weakness, and cardiac palpitations.

### Carbon Dioxide / Bicarbonate (CO2)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 22 to 29 mEq/L
- **What it measures:** The buffering capacity of blood and metabolic acid-base status.

### Calcium
- **Standard Reference Range:** 8.5 to 10.2 mg/dL
- **Clinical Significance:** Crucial for bone health, neuromuscular transmission, and cardiac contractility. Regulated by parathyroid hormone (PTH) and Vitamin D.

## 4. Blood Sugar / Glycemic Marker

### Fasting Blood Glucose
- **Standard Reference Range:** 70 to 99 mg/dL (after an 8-hour overnight fast)
- **Impaired Fasting Glucose (Prediabetes):** 100 to 125 mg/dL
- **Diabetes Diagnostic Threshold:** 126 mg/dL or higher on two separate occasions
- **Hypoglycemia (< 70 mg/dL):** Causes shakiness, sweating, anxiety, confusion. Immediate carbohydrate ingestion required.

## 5. Liver Function & Protein Biomarkers

### Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 7 to 56 U/L
- **Significance:** Enzyme primarily found in liver cells (hepatocytes). Released into the bloodstream during liver inflammation or injury.
- **Mild Elevation (1x to 3x normal):** Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), alcohol consumption, vigorous unaccustomed exercise, statin medications, or acetaminophen use.
- **Marked Elevation (> 10x normal):** Acute viral hepatitis, ischemic liver injury, or toxin ingestion.

### Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 10 to 40 U/L
- **Significance:** Found in liver, heart, and skeletal muscle. An AST/ALT ratio > 2.0 often suggests alcohol-induced liver stress.

### Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 44 to 147 U/L
- **Significance:** Found in bile ducts and bone. High ALP with elevated GGT indicates biliary blockage; high ALP with normal GGT suggests bone remodeling/growth.

### Total Bilirubin
- **Standard Reference Range:** 0.2 to 1.2 mg/dL
- **Significance:** Byproduct of red blood cell breakdown. Levels > 2.5 mg/dL cause jaundice (yellowing of eyes and skin). Mild isolated elevation (1.3 - 2.0 mg/dL) with normal liver enzymes is commonly Gilbert's syndrome, a benign genetic trait.

### Albumin & Total Protein
- **Albumin Reference Range:** 3.4 to 5.4 g/dL
- **Significance:** Main protein produced by the liver, maintains oncotic pressure. Low albumin may reflect chronic illness, malnutrition, nephrotic syndrome, or chronic liver disease.
