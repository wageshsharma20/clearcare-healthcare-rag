# Clinical Reference Guide: Thyroid Function Panel & Endocrine Markers
*Source: American Thyroid Association (ATA) & Endocrine Society Guidelines*
*Target Audience: Patient Guidance & Primary Care Interpretation*

## 1. Overview of the Thyroid Panel
The thyroid is a butterfly-shaped gland located in the lower front of the neck. It produces hormones that regulate metabolism, heart rate, body temperature, and energy consumption throughout the body.

---

## 2. Primary Thyroid Biomarkers

### Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 0.45 to 4.50 mIU/L (uIU/mL)
- **What it measures:** A pituitary hormone that tells the thyroid gland how much thyroid hormone to produce. TSH has an *inverse* relationship with thyroid activity (like a thermostat).
- **High TSH (> 4.50 mIU/L) — Underactive Thyroid (Hypothyroidism):**
  - The pituitary is shouting at the thyroid because circulating thyroid hormone is too low.
  - **Common Symptoms:** Chronic fatigue, weight gain despite normal diet, cold intolerance, constipation, brain fog, brittle nails, hair thinning, depression.
  - **Most Common Cause:** Hashimoto's thyroiditis (autoimmune destruction of thyroid tissue).
  - **Subclinical Hypothyroidism:** TSH is mildly elevated (4.5 - 10.0 mIU/L) while Free T4 remains in normal range. Often monitored unless symptomatic or pregnant.
- **Low TSH (< 0.40 mIU/L) — Overactive Thyroid (Hyperthyroidism):**
  - The pituitary stops signaling because the body is flooded with excess thyroid hormone.
  - **Common Symptoms:** Rapid heart rate (tachycardia), anxiety, tremors, heat intolerance, unexplained weight loss, diarrhea, insomnia.
  - **Most Common Cause:** Graves' disease or toxic multinodular goiter.

### Free Thyroxine (Free T4)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 0.8 to 1.8 ng/dL
- **Significance:** The active, unbound form of T4 circulating in the blood. Free T4 directly reflects the hormone available to body tissues.
- **Low Free T4 + High TSH:** Confirms overt primary hypothyroidism. Treatment typically involves oral Levothyroxine (Synthroid), taken on an empty stomach 30-60 minutes before breakfast with a full glass of water.

### Free Triiodothyronine (Free T3)
- **Standard Reference Range:** 2.3 to 4.2 pg/mL
- **Significance:** The most biologically potent active thyroid hormone, largely converted from T4 in peripheral tissues (liver and kidneys).
